# Antitect Site — Copy

Voice: plain, declarative, second person. Specific over aspirational. Slightly stern — the brand's own line is *"Learning should lead to the ability to do."*

Never: unlock, empower, transform, revolutionise, journey, unleash, "endless possibilities." No exclamation marks, no emoji, no tracked-out capitals as labels, no manufactured urgency.

---

## Navbar

```
ANTITECT    Programmes   Events   How it works   About    [ Join the community ]
```

Four items, one action. The community button is evergreen — events come and go, and a nav button with nothing behind it is worse than no button.

Not included: Blog, Resources, Pricing, Contact. No blog exists, price lives on the programme page, contact belongs in the footer.

**Mobile:** logo, hamburger, and the community button stays visible. It is the one thing that should never require a tap to reach.

## Hero

**Headline — three options**

1. *Learn AI by building with it.*
   Clearest. Legible in two seconds, states the method, carries the search term. Safest choice.
2. *Build with AI. Not just about it.*
   Sharper and more memorable. Slightly more work to parse, and the distinction may be lost on a cold visitor.
3. *What will you have built by the end of it?*
   Most distinctive, puts the outcome in the reader's hands. Risks sounding like a challenge before anyone knows who you are.

Recommended: **option 1**, with the confrontational register saved for the section below it.

**Subhead**
> Antitect runs hands-on AI programmes in Nigeria for people who want to leave with something they built — not notes they will never open again.

**Primary action:** `Join the community`
**Secondary action:** `See what's coming up`

**Right panel** — the next event, or the programme if no event is scheduled:

> NEXT UP

> [ Register free ]

If nothing is scheduled, the panel carries the programme instead. It is never empty and never says "coming soon."

## The premise

**Headline**
> You have watched the tutorials. What have you built?

**Body**
> Most AI learning ends the same way. You finish the course, you understand the concepts, and nothing in your work actually changes. You cannot point at anything.
>
> Antitect is built the other way round. You start with a project you need to build. You learn what that project requires. An instructor reviews what you make and tells you how to make it better. You finish when it works.
>
> The question at the end is not what you learned. It is what you can now do.

This is where the site separates itself. Without naming the problem, "project-based" is just a feature nobody weighs.

## How it works

**Headline**
> Learn. Build. Review. Improve. Apply.

**Intro**
> Every Antitect programme runs on the same loop. It is the reason people finish with working projects instead of half-watched videos.

**Learn**
> You cover what the project in front of you actually needs. Not a syllabus you work through in order.

**Build**
> You build the thing. This is most of the time you spend.

**Review**
> An instructor sits with your work for thirty minutes, one to one, every week. They tell you what is wrong with it.

**Improve**
> You fix it and build it again. This is where the capability actually forms.

**Apply**
> You use it. In your business, your job, or your portfolio.

**Headline**

**Intro**
> Longer, structured programmes for people who want to develop a capability properly rather than sample it.

**AI Automation Applied**

> You build automations that do real work in your business or your job — handling enquiries, processing documents, moving information between the tools you already use.
>
> Live sessions each week, plus a thirty-minute one-to-one project review. You progress by finishing projects, not by attending.
>

**What you leave with**
> - Working automations running in your own business or workflow
> - A portfolio of completed projects you can show
> - The ability to build the next one without us

**Price and dates — pulled from the API, never written into the page**

> [price] · Next cohort starts [date]

Prices change between cohorts and differ across programmes and workshops. Nothing in the copy should imply a fixed or permanent figure — no "just ₦100,000", no "our ₦100,000 programme", no price in a headline, meta description, or share image. The page renders whatever the CRM holds for that programme, formatted with a naira symbol and thousands separators.

The same applies to duration and session count: a future programme may not be six weeks. Where copy above names "six weeks" or "three live sessions a week", those come from the programme record too. Only the *shape* of the sentence is fixed.

**Action:** `Enrol`

The weekly time commitment is stated plainly on purpose. It builds trust and filters out the people who would drop out in week two and ask for a refund.

## From the first cohort

Workshop 001 ran on 1 August in Lagos. That means real attendees, real photographs, and — if it went well — real outcomes. This is the single highest-value content you can gather this week, and it cannot be written; it has to be collected.

**Placement matters as much as content.** Do not put all the proof in one block at the bottom where people stop reading. Distribute it:

- **One short quote directly under the hero**, where the first doubt forms
- **A number in the premise section**, if you have one worth stating
- **Two or three fuller quotes on the programme page**, near the enrol action, where the real decision happens
- **Photographs throughout**, replacing every piece of stock imagery

If you have a countable outcome, state it plainly:
> [N] people built and deployed a working AI application in a single session.

**Ask three or four attendees for one specific sentence each** — what they built, not how they felt. "I built an automation that sorts my customer enquiries and it still runs" is worth ten quotes saying the workshop was insightful.

Do not build this section until the material is real. No stock photographs, no invented quotes, no grey placeholder logo boxes.

## SEO

Your audience searches before it browses. Someone typing "AI course Nigeria" into Google has more intent than anyone who will ever see a social post, and right now that traffic goes to competitors.

**Primary keyword:** *practical AI training Nigeria*
**Secondary:** *AI automation course*, *learn AI Lagos*, *hands-on AI workshop*, *AI training for professionals Africa*

Place the primary term in the page title, the first paragraph of body copy, and one subheading. Never more than that — repeating it reads badly to humans and no longer helps with ranking.

**Location matters more than the mission statement here.** "Africa" is the vision; "Nigeria" and "Lagos" are what people actually type. Use the specific terms in metadata and body copy, and save the continental framing for the About page and the footer.

### Meta titles and descriptions

Keep descriptions under 160 characters or they get truncated.

**Home**
> Practical AI Training in Nigeria — Antitect
>
> Hands-on AI programmes in Lagos. Build real projects with instructor guidance — free webinars, workshops, and structured capability programmes.

**Events list**
> Upcoming AI Events and Workshops — Antitect
>
> Free AI webinars and hands-on workshops in Lagos and online. See what's coming up and reserve a place.

**Programmes list**
> AI Capability Development Programmes — Antitect
>
> Structured, instructor-led AI programmes with weekly one-to-one project reviews. Built for working professionals in Nigeria.

**About**
> About Antitect — Building Practical AI Capability Across Africa
>
> Who we are, how we teach, and why we believe learning should lead to the ability to do.

Event and programme pages pull their title, description and image from the CRM. Those matter more than the homepage's, since every shared WhatsApp link points at one of them. **Never put a price in a meta description or share image** — it gets cached and served long after it changes.

Drop the `meta-keywords` tag from the current site. Search engines have ignored it for over a decade.

### Internal linking

Search engines and readers both follow the same path, so make it explicit:

- Home → the programme page, the events list, About
- Every event page → the programme page. The webinar is the funnel; make the next step reachable without returning home.
- Programme page → About, for the instructor
- Footer → everything, on every page

### Image alt text

Every image earns a description. The Workshop 001 photographs are the highest-value ones you have:

> "Participants building AI applications at Antitect's first workshop in Lagos"

Not "workshop photo", not "IMG_4821". Alt text is read by screen readers, indexed by image search, and displayed when an image fails to load — which on a slow Nigerian mobile connection is often.

Name the files properly too: `antitect-workshop-lagos-participants.jpg`, not `hero-group.jpg`.

## Applied voice and tone

Plain, declarative, second person, slightly stern — taken directly from the brand's own line, *"Learning should lead to the ability to do."* Confident without hype, because the audience is saturated with hype and restraint reads as credibility.

The register shifts once: declarative in the hero for immediate clarity, confrontational in the premise section once the reader is already engaged.

## Copy rules

- **Second person.** "You build," never "participants develop capabilities."
- **Buttons name what happens.** "Register free", "Enrol", "Join the community". Never "Submit", "Learn more", "Get started".
- **An action keeps its name through the flow.** A "Register" button produces a "Registered" confirmation.
- **Specific beats aspirational.** "You build an automation that handles your customer enquiries" beats "unlock the power of AI."
- **Be honest about the demand.** State the weekly time commitment and the price plainly and early, whatever they are for that cohort.
- **Never hardcode a price, duration, or date.** They come from the CRM. A figure written into the page survives long after it changes, and a stale price is worse than no price.
- **Sentence case for headlines.** No colouring or bolding a single word inside a headline for emphasis.
- **Errors say what happened and what to do.** They do not apologise and they are never vague.
